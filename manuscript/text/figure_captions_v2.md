# Figure captions

**Figure 1.** Insurance simulations under mixed versus aligned responses. Community variability and realised growth synchrony for mixed response slopes (directionally diverse) versus positive response slopes (aligned). **(A)** Heatmap of (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}}) across innovation SD (\sigma*{\mathrm{env}}) (columns) and imposed environmental correlation (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}) (rows) for mixed slopes with (S=4); darker colours indicate lower variability. **(B)** As in (A) but for positive slopes with (S=4), showing increasing (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}}) with both (\sigma*{\mathrm{env}}) and (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}). **(C)** (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}}) versus (\rho*{\mathrm{env}}) at (\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5) for (S=4) and (S=8); points show replicate values and lines show scenario means (mixed: solid; positive: dashed). **(D)** Realised growth synchrony (mean pairwise correlation of per-capita growth rates) versus imposed driver correlation (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}); the dashed line indicates equality ((y=x)). For (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}\ge 0), mixed slopes yield much lower realised synchrony than imposed correlation and often negative growth-rate correlations, whereas positive slopes maintain high realised synchrony that increases with (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}).

**Figure 2.** Richness effects on community variability under mixed response directions. Scenario means of (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}}) as a function of species richness (S) for mixed-slope communities, averaged over the shared non-negative imposed-correlation subset ((\rho_{\mathrm{env}}\ge 0)) for each (\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}). For (\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}>0), (\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}) declines sharply from (S=2) to (S=4) and continues to decrease towards (S=8); the (\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0) baseline remains near zero across richness.

**Figure 3.** Imposed versus realised synchrony by slope regime. Mean pairwise correlations of per-capita growth rates ((\mathrm{corr}*{\mathrm{realised}})) as a function of imposed driver correlation (\rho*{\mathrm{env}}) at (\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5). **(A)** Mixed slopes for (S=2,4,8): realised growth correlations decline strongly as (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}) increases and become negative at high (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}) for low richness. **(B)** Positive slopes for (S=4,8): realised correlations increase with (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}) and remain high. The dashed line indicates equality ((y=x)). **(C)** Difference (\Delta\mathrm{corr}=\mathrm{corr}*{\mathrm{realised}}-\rho*{\mathrm{env}}), shown as richness-averaged regime means: deviations become strongly negative at high (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}) in the mixed-slope regime, while the positive-slope regime shows positive deviations at low (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}) and near-zero to modestly negative deviations at high (\rho_{\mathrm{env}}).

**Figure 4.** Community variability versus synchrony metrics in mixed-slope communities. Relationships between scenario mean community variability and synchrony across mixed-slope simulations (post–burn-in, averaged across replicates). **(A)** (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}}) versus Loreau–de Mazancourt synchrony (\phi*{\mathrm{LdM}}). **(B)** (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}}) versus mean pairwise correlation of per-capita growth rates. Colours indicate species richness ((S=2,4,8)). Low synchrony and weak/negative growth-rate correlations are associated with low (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}}), whereas higher synchrony is associated with higher community variability.

**Figure 5.** Sensitivity of mixed-slope insurance patterns to environmental persistence. Community variability (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}}) versus imposed driver correlation (\rho*{\mathrm{env}}) at (\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5) for mixed-slope communities, shown for (S=2) (left) and (S=4) (right) across four values of temporal autocorrelation (\phi) ((0, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95)). Points show replicate values and lines show scenario means. Legends report (\phi) and the corresponding stationary environmental standard deviation (\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}/\sqrt{1-\phi^2}). Increasing (\phi) increases (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}}) (via greater persistence and higher stationary variance), while the richness effect and the generally negative (\mathrm{CV}*{\mathrm{tot}})–(\rho_{\mathrm{env}}) association in the mixed-slope regime remain evident.
