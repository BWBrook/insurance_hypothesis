## Significance Statement

Environmental variability can destabilise populations, yet biodiversity is often expected to stabilise ecosystem functioning through insurance. This study uses a deliberately simple stochastic community model to show when that expectation should, and should not, hold. Simulations separate imposed environmental correlation from realised synchrony in population growth. Strong compensatory insurance emerges only when species differ in response direction, so that shared environmental anomalies create compensatory growth. When all species respond in the same direction, shared forcing produces synchrony and high community variability despite greater richness. The result clarifies why richness alone is an incomplete predictor of stability, and highlights response-direction diversity as a key condition for biodiversity insurance under environmental change.
