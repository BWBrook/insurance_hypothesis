## Significance Statement

Perfectly balanced opposing responses do not guarantee that sharing an environment stabilises a community. In stochastic Ricker communities, shared exposure can double mean squared community CV relative to independent exposures while compensatory fluctuations persist. A second-order calculation links the initial loss of buffering to changes in community composition. Its coefficient, calibrated on earlier simulations, also predicts nonlinear ratios and reversals at new competition and autocorrelation values: all 35 evaluable ratios were predicted within 5%, and all 17 evaluable eight-species buffering or amplification calls were correct. The result gives response diversity a sharper, testable interpretation: its stability benefit depends on fluctuation size and population feedback as well as on the balance of species' responses.
