## Significance Statement

Opposing species responses can buffer community fluctuations, but their contributions to total abundance determine how strongly they compensate. This manuscript expresses that dependence as an abundance-weighted forcing benchmark built from established covariance theory, then tests it with stochastic multispecies Ricker simulations. Reversing response signs while holding every magnitude fixed reverses the effect of exposure correlation, and continuous changes in response balance and species contributions produce a broad transition near the benchmark. The nonlinear limits are informative: at balanced responses, mean squared coefficients of variation remain 0.32 of the independent-exposure baseline, while near the rotation threshold the simulation shows weak amplification where the benchmark predicts weak buffering. For *Oikos* readers, the result identifies three quantities to measure together: exposure covariance, species response slopes, and species contributions to the aggregate.
