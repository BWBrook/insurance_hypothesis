# Figure captions (v2)

**Figure 1.** Environmental variability destabilises aligned responses more strongly than mixed responses. Production-scale S=4 simulations use 1000 replicates per scenario. **(A)** Heatmap of mean community variability ($\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$) for aligned positive slopes across innovation SD $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ and effective driver correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. **(B)** Mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ increases, comparing mixed response directions, aligned response directions, and the aligned-minus-mixed gap. Faint bands show the range across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The shaded high-$\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ region is treated as a stress extension rather than the core low-stress inference regime.

**Figure 2.** Richness effects on community variability under mixed response directions. Production-scale mixed-slope simulations show scenario means of $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ as a function of species richness $S$, averaged over the shared non-negative effective-correlation subset ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}\ge 0$) for each $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$; faint bands show the range across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Over the examined richness range ($S=2$-8), $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ declines strongly for all $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}>0$, while the $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0$ baseline remains near zero.

**Figure 3.** Imposed versus realised synchrony by slope regime at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$. Production-scale simulations use 1000 replicates per scenario. **(A)** Mixed slopes ($S=2,4,8$): realised per-capita log-growth correlations fall well below imposed driver correlation as $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ increases, becoming strongly negative at low richness. **(B)** Positive slopes ($S=2,4,8$): realised log-growth correlations increase with $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and remain strongly positive. Dashed lines indicate equality ($y=x$). **(C)** Difference metric $\Delta\mathrm{corr} = \mathrm{corr}_{\mathrm{realised}} - \rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$, shown as richness-averaged regime means with bands spanning the richness range. Mixed slopes become strongly negative at high imposed correlation, whereas positive slopes remain near zero to modestly negative.

**Figure 4.** Community variability versus synchrony metrics in mixed-slope communities. Production-scale scenario means are shown from the mixed-slope richness grid, excluding the deterministic $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0$ baseline. **(A)** $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ versus Loreau-de Mazancourt synchrony $\phi_{\mathrm{LdM}}$. **(B)** $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ versus mean pairwise realised per-capita log-growth correlation. Colour indicates species richness $S$ and point size indicates $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$. Lower abundance synchrony and weaker/negative realised log-growth correlations correspond to lower community variability.

**Figure 5.** Sensitivity of mixed-slope insurance patterns across a persistence–stationary-variance stress gradient. Production-scale scenario means use 1000 replicates per scenario. Community variability ($\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$) is shown versus effective driver correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ on x-axis) at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$ for **(A)** $S=2$ and **(B)** $S=4$ across $\phi\in\{0,0.7,0.9,0.95\}$. Legend entries report $\phi$ with corresponding stationary environmental SD, $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}/\sqrt{1-\phi^2}$. Increasing $\phi$ inflates variability by increasing both persistence and stationary variance, but the mixed-slope richness contrast remains visible.

**Figure S3.** Production positive-matched control (magnitude-matched aligned slopes). This focused 1000-replicate control compares mixed, positive-matched, and aligned-positive slope regimes at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$ for $S=4$ and $S=8$. **(A)** Mean community variability ($\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$) as a function of effective driver correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$). **(B)** Mean pairwise realised per-capita log-growth correlation over the same gradient. The positive-matched control isolates response sign structure from slope-magnitude differences: it sits well below the fully aligned-positive regime for community variability, while realised log-growth correlations remain much closer to aligned responses than to mixed responses.

**Figure S4.** Production species-level CV diagnostic. Approximate $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ (computed from `mean_species_var` and `mean_species_mean`) is shown versus effective driver correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$, using the same 1000-replicate production grid as Figure 3 for mixed and positive slope regimes at $S=4,8$. This panel documents species-level variability alongside community-level variability and synchrony metrics.
