# Figure captions (v2)

**Figure 1.** Environmental variability destabilises aligned responses more strongly than mixed responses. Simulations for $S=4$ use 1000 replicates per scenario. Panel A shows mean community variability ($\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$) for aligned positive slopes across innovation SD $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ and effective driver correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Panel B shows mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ increases, comparing mixed response directions, aligned response directions, and the aligned-minus-mixed gap; faint bands span the range across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The dotted line marks the upper edge of the core grid at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=2$, and shading marks the highest-stress region ($\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}\ge3$).

**Figure 2.** Richness effects on community variability under mixed response directions. Scenario means of $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ from 1000-replicate simulations are shown as a function of species richness $S$, averaged over the shared non-negative effective-correlation subset ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}\ge 0$) for each $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$; faint bands show the range across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Over the examined richness range ($S=2$-8), $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ declines strongly for all $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}>0$, while the $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0$ baseline remains near zero.

**Figure 3.** Imposed versus realised synchrony by slope regime at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$. Simulations use 1000 replicates per scenario. Panel A shows mixed slopes ($S=2,4,8$): realised per-capita log-growth correlations fall well below imposed driver correlation as $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ increases, becoming strongly negative at low richness. Panel B shows positive slopes ($S=2,4,8$): realised log-growth correlations increase with $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and remain strongly positive. Dashed lines indicate equality ($y=x$). Panel C shows the difference metric $\Delta\mathrm{corr} = \mathrm{corr}_{\mathrm{realised}} - \rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as richness-averaged regime means with bands spanning the richness range. Mixed slopes become strongly negative at high imposed correlation; positive slopes are positive at low imposed correlation, near zero at intermediate values, and modestly negative only at the high end.

**Figure 4.** Community variability versus synchrony metrics in mixed-slope communities. Scenario means are shown from the 1000-replicate mixed-slope richness grid, excluding the deterministic $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0$ baseline. Panel A plots $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ against Loreau-de Mazancourt synchrony $\phi_{\mathrm{LdM}}$; these metrics share $\operatorname{Var}(N_{\mathrm{tot}})$ and their association is partly mathematical. Panel B plots $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ against mean pairwise realised per-capita log-growth correlation. Colour indicates species richness $S$ and point size indicates $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$. The panels show raw scenario-level associations, not independent effects or mediation.

**Figure 5.** Sensitivity of mixed-slope insurance patterns across a persistence–stationary-variance stress gradient. Scenario means use 1000 replicates per scenario. Community variability ($\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$) is shown versus effective driver correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ on x-axis) at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$ for $S=2$ (panel A) and $S=4$ (panel B) across $\phi\in\{0,0.7,0.9,0.95\}$. Legend entries report $\phi$ with corresponding stationary environmental SD, $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}/\sqrt{1-\phi^2}$. Increasing $\phi$ inflates variability by increasing persistence and stationary variance together, but the mixed-slope richness contrast remains visible.

**Figure S1.** Mixed-slope heatmap summary for community variability. Heatmaps of mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ are shown across effective driver correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$; rows) and innovation SD ($\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$; columns) for $S=2,4,8$, using the same 1000-replicate mixed-slope grid as Figures 2 and 4. A single shared colour scale and one colourbar are used across panels.

**Figure S2.** Mixed-slope heatmap summary for realised community synchrony. Heatmaps of mean Loreau-de Mazancourt synchrony $\phi_{\mathrm{LdM}}$ are shown across effective driver correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$; rows) and innovation SD ($\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$; columns) for $S=2,4,8$, using the same 1000-replicate mixed-slope grid as Figures 2 and 4. A shared colour scale and one colourbar are used across panels.

**Figure S3.** Positive-matched control (magnitude-matched aligned slopes). This focused 1000-replicate control compares mixed, positive-matched, and aligned-positive slope regimes at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$ for $S=4$ and $S=8$. Panel A shows mean community variability ($\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$) as a function of effective driver correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$). Panel B shows mean pairwise realised per-capita log-growth correlation over the same gradient. The positive-matched control isolates response sign structure from slope-magnitude differences: it sits well below the fully aligned-positive regime for community variability, but its realised log-growth correlations rise with $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and approach the aligned-response pattern.

**Figure S4.** Species-level CV diagnostic. Approximate $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\sqrt{S^{-1}\sum_i v_i}/(S^{-1}\sum_i m_i)$ is shown against effective driver correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$, where $m_i$ and $v_i$ are the temporal mean and variance of species $i$. The 1000-replicate grid matches Figure 3 for mixed and positive slope regimes at $S=4,8$.
