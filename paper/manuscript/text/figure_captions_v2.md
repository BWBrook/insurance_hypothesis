# Figure captions (v2)

**Figure 1.** Abundance-weighted response balance and the transition between buffering and amplification. Panel A compares mixed responses (legend: balanced mixed) with matched aligned responses (legend: aligned, matched magnitude) at $S=4$ and 8. Panel B plots the ratio of mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}^2$ under near-perfect exposure correlation to that under independent exposures against the abundance-weighted alignment index $A$, for a fixed-norm rotation from the mixed to the matched aligned response vector and for an unequal-$K_i$ path that holds deterministic total abundance constant, both at $S=4$. Error bars are 95% Monte Carlo intervals. The dotted line is the first-order forcing benchmark, $(1-\rho_{\mathrm{eff}})+\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}SA$; nonlinear ratios depart from both its magnitude and, near the rotation threshold, its predicted direction. Dashed lines mark $A=1/S=0.25$ and a ratio of one. The inset enlarges the near-threshold comparison. Panel C shows the same ratio for the mixed and matched aligned endpoints across $r_0$. Each scenario uses 1000 replicates, $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$, and $\phi=0.4$.

**Figure 2.** Exposure correlation versus realised synchrony for mixed and steep aligned responses at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$, with 1000 replicates per scenario. Panel A, mixed responses ($S=2,4,8$): realised per-capita log-growth correlations fall well below the effective exposure correlation as $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ increases and become strongly negative at low richness. Panel B, steep aligned responses ($S=2,4,8$): realised correlations increase with $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and remain strongly positive. Dashed lines show equality ($y=x$). Panel C shows $\mathrm{corr}_{\mathrm{realised}}-\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ averaged over richness for each treatment, with bands spanning the richness range.

**Figure 3.** Environmental variability destabilises steep aligned responses more strongly than mixed responses. Steep aligned slopes span $[0.1,1.0]$ and mixed slopes span $[-0.5,0.5]$, so this contrast changes response magnitude as well as direction. Simulations for $S=4$ use 1000 replicates per scenario. Panel A shows mean community variability ($\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$) for steep aligned responses across innovation SD $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ and effective exposure correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Panel B shows mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ increases for mixed responses, steep aligned responses, and their difference; faint bands span the range across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The dotted line marks the upper edge of the core grid at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=2$, and shading marks the highest-stress region ($\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}\ge3$).

**Figure 4.** Community variability along the deterministic mixed-response assembly sequence. Scenario means of $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ are shown against species richness $S$, averaged over the shared non-negative effective-correlation values for each $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$; faint bands span those correlation values. Each step adds evenly spaced slopes within $[-0.5,0.5]$, so response composition and root-mean-square slope change with $S$. The figure describes this assembly sequence, not an isolated causal effect of richness.

**Figure 5.** Mixed-response assembly patterns across a fixed-innovation stress gradient. Mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is shown against effective exposure correlation at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$ for $S=2$ (panel A) and $S=4$ (panel B) across $\phi\in\{0,0.7,0.9,0.95\}$. Legend entries give $\phi$ and the stationary exposure SD, $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}/\sqrt{1-\phi^2}$. Increasing $\phi$ changes temporal autocorrelation and stationary variance together; the figure does not isolate either effect.

**Figure S1.** Mixed-response heatmap summary for community variability. Heatmaps of mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ are shown across effective exposure correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$; rows) and innovation SD ($\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$; columns) for $S=2,4,8$, using the same assembly grid as Fig. 4. A single shared colour scale and one colourbar are used across panels.

**Figure S2.** Mixed-response heatmap summary for realised community synchrony. Heatmaps of mean Loreau–de Mazancourt synchrony $\phi_{\mathrm{LdM}}$ are shown across effective exposure correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$; rows) and innovation SD ($\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$; columns) for $S=2,4,8$, using the same assembly grid as Fig. 4. A shared colour scale and one colourbar are used across panels. Pale-grey cells at zero environmental variability are undefined: the post-burn-in abundances are constant, so synchrony cannot be estimated.

**Figure S3.** Matched aligned control. This 1000-replicate control compares mixed, matched aligned (legend: positive matched) and steep aligned (legend: aligned positive) responses at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$ for $S=4$ and 8. Panel A shows mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ against effective exposure correlation; panel B shows mean pairwise realised per-capita log-growth correlation. The matched aligned treatment separates response sign from slope magnitude.

**Figure S4.** Species-level CV diagnostic. Approximate $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\sqrt{S^{-1}\sum_i v_i}/(S^{-1}\sum_i m_i)$ is shown against effective exposure correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$, where $m_i$ and $v_i$ are the temporal mean and variance of species $i$. The grid matches Fig. 2 for mixed and steep aligned responses at $S=4,8$.
