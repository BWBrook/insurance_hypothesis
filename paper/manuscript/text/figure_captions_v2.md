# Figure captions (v2)

**Figure 1.** Abundance-weighted response balance and the transition between buffering and amplification. (A) Mean community variability, $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$, against effective exposure correlation for mixed responses (legend: balanced mixed) and matched aligned responses (legend: aligned, matched magnitude) at $S=4$ and 8. (B) Ratio of mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}^2$ under near-perfect exposure correlation to that under independent exposures, plotted against the abundance-weighted alignment index $A$ for a fixed-norm rotation from the mixed to the matched aligned response vector (legend: response alignment) and for an unequal-$K_i$ path that holds deterministic total abundance constant (legend: abundance weighting), both at $S=4$. Ratios below one indicate buffering by correlated exposure, and ratios above one indicate amplification. Error bars are 95% Monte Carlo intervals. The dotted line is the first-order forcing benchmark, $(1-\rho_{\mathrm{eff}})+\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}SA$, from which the nonlinear ratios depart in magnitude and, near the rotation threshold, in direction. Dashed lines mark $A=1/S=0.25$ and a ratio of one, and the inset enlarges the near-threshold comparison. (C) The same ratio for the mixed and matched aligned endpoints across intrinsic growth rates $r_0$. Each scenario uses 1000 replicates, $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$, and $\phi=0.4$.

**Figure 2.** Imposed exposure correlation versus realised synchrony for mixed and steep aligned responses at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$, with 1000 replicates per scenario. (A) In mixed communities ($S=2,4,8$), realised per-capita log-growth correlations fall well below the effective exposure correlation as $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ increases, becoming strongly negative at low richness. (B) In steep aligned communities ($S=2,4,8$), realised correlations increase with $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and remain strongly positive. Dashed lines in (A) and (B) show equality ($y=x$), where realised synchrony would simply track the imposed correlation. (C) The difference $\mathrm{corr}_{\mathrm{realised}}-\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$, averaged over richness for mixed and steep aligned (legend: aligned responses) communities, with bands spanning the richness range; the dashed line marks zero difference.

**Figure 3.** Environmental variability destabilises steep aligned communities more strongly than mixed communities. Steep aligned slopes (legend: aligned responses) span $[0.1,1.0]$ and mixed slopes span $[-0.5,0.5]$, so this contrast changes response magnitude as well as direction. Simulations for $S=4$ use 1000 replicates per scenario. (A) Mean community variability, $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$, of steep aligned communities across innovation SD $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ and effective exposure correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. (B) Mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$, averaged across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$, as $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ increases, for mixed responses, steep aligned responses, and their difference; faint bands span the range across $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Dotted lines mark the upper edge of the core grid at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=2$, and shading in (B) marks the highest-stress region ($\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}\ge3$).

**Figure 4.** Community variability along the deterministic mixed-response assembly sequence. Scenario means of $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ are plotted against species richness $S$ for each $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$, averaged over the shared non-negative effective-correlation values; faint bands span those correlation values. At each richness the slopes are evenly spaced within $[-0.5,0.5]$, so response composition and root-mean-square slope change with $S$, and the figure describes this assembly sequence rather than an isolated causal effect of richness.

**Figure 5.** Mixed-response assembly patterns across a fixed-innovation stress gradient. Mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is plotted against effective exposure correlation at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$ for (A) $S=2$ and (B) $S=4$, at $\phi\in\{0,0.7,0.9,0.95\}$. Legend entries give $\phi$ and the stationary exposure SD, $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}/\sqrt{1-\phi^2}$. Because increasing $\phi$ raises temporal autocorrelation and stationary variance together, the figure does not isolate either effect.

**Figure S1.** Mixed-response heatmap summary for community variability. Mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ across effective exposure correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$; rows) and innovation SD ($\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$; columns) for $S=2,4,8$, from the same assembly grid as Fig. 4. All panels share a single colour scale and colourbar.

**Figure S2.** Mixed-response heatmap summary for realised community synchrony. Mean Loreau–de Mazancourt synchrony $\phi_{\mathrm{LdM}}$ across effective exposure correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$; rows) and innovation SD ($\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$; columns) for $S=2,4,8$, from the same assembly grid as Fig. 4. All panels share a single colour scale and colourbar. Pale-grey cells at zero environmental variability are undefined, because post-burn-in abundances are then constant and synchrony cannot be estimated.

**Figure S3.** Matched aligned control. This 1000-replicate control compares mixed, matched aligned (legend: positive matched), and steep aligned (legend: aligned positive) responses at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$ for $S=4$ and 8. (A) Mean $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ against effective exposure correlation. (B) Mean pairwise realised per-capita log-growth correlation against effective exposure correlation. The matched aligned treatment separates response sign from slope magnitude.

**Figure S4.** Species-level CV diagnostic. Approximate $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\sqrt{S^{-1}\sum_i v_i}/(S^{-1}\sum_i m_i)$ against effective exposure correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$, where $m_i$ and $v_i$ are the temporal mean and variance of species $i$. The grid matches Fig. 2 for mixed responses and steep aligned responses (legend: aligned responses) at $S=4,8$.
