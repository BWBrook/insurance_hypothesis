# Cover Letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider **"Shared environments can destabilise communities with balanced opposing responses"** as a research paper in *Oikos*.

When the same environmental fluctuation favours one species and harms another, sharing an environment can strengthen compensation and stabilise their combined abundance. This paper asks how far that protection extends. In stochastic Ricker communities, shared exposure can instead increase community variability even when opposing responses are perfectly balanced. In one example, it increases mean squared community CV by 46%; the reversal persists in trajectories ten times longer. Negative covariance remains, so compensation survives even as environmental correlation becomes destabilising.

A sign-only control separates response direction from response magnitude. A second-order calculation then predicts the initial departure from perfect cancellation: its predictions agree within 4% across 23 prespecified small-fluctuation settings. Its failure at larger fluctuations defines the limit of that explanation, while the nonlinear simulations establish the reversal. The resulting ecological distinction is useful beyond this model: opposing responses can remain advantageous relative to aligned responses even when sharing an environment no longer adds stability.

The paper connects response diversity to an explicit dimension of stability, a priority identified by Ross and colleagues in *Oikos*. It builds on established covariance and population-feedback theory and makes a testable prediction about how fluctuation size changes the benefit of shared exposure. Response balance, species contributions and population feedback must be considered together.

The study uses no empirical data, field sampling, live animals, or human participants. A de-identified, uv-managed reproducibility package will accompany submission, allowing reviewers and editors to inspect the code, tests, simulation specifications, compact summaries, and figure/table workflow. The final scientific release will be maintained in a clean, versioned public repository and archived in Zenodo with a DOI.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere and follows the journal's double-anonymised review process; author-identifying information appears only in the title page and editor-facing materials.

Thank you for considering this manuscript for publication in *Oikos*.

Sincerely,

Barry W. Brook
