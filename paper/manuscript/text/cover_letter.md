# Cover Letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider **"Shared environments can destabilise communities with balanced opposing responses"** as a research paper in *Oikos*.

When the same environmental fluctuation favours one species and harms another, sharing an environment should help their abundances compensate. This paper shows how that protection can reverse. In stochastic Ricker communities with perfectly balanced opposing responses, shared exposure can double the mean squared coefficient of variation of total abundance relative to independent exposures. Species still partly offset one another: the reversal concerns the extra effect of sharing an environment, not the loss of response diversity's benefit relative to aligned responses.

The paper connects this reversal to a mechanism and a prospective prediction. Opposing responses cancel the direct environmental input to total abundance while leaving species displaced from equilibrium. Nonlinear growth transmits changes in that compositional displacement back to the total. A second-order calculation predicts the initial loss of buffering within 4% across 23 prespecified small-fluctuation settings. Its coefficient also provides a useful axis beyond that limit: after calibration on the earlier simulations, interpolation along this axis predicted all 35 evaluable ratios at new competition and autocorrelation values within 5%, and correctly identified buffering or amplification in all 17 evaluable eight-species settings.

For *Oikos* readers, the contribution is a testable connection between response diversity, population feedback and a specified dimension of community stability. The result distinguishes the local analytical prediction from the calibrated nonlinear prediction, and confines both to the model and parameter domain tested. It shows why opposing responses alone cannot determine whether a shared environment adds stability.

The study uses no empirical data, field sampling, live animals, or human participants. A de-identified, uv-managed reproducibility package will accompany submission, allowing reviewers and editors to inspect the code, tests, simulation specifications, compact summaries, and figure/table workflow. The final scientific release will be maintained in a clean, versioned public repository and archived in Zenodo with a DOI.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere and follows the journal's double-anonymised review process; author-identifying information appears only in the title page and editor-facing materials.

Thank you for considering this manuscript for publication in *Oikos*.

Sincerely,

Barry W. Brook
